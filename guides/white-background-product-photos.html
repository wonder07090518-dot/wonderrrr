How to get a clean white background product photo

A clean white background gives a product room to stand out, but getting there starts before you open an editor. Light the item evenly, capture its real edges and keep a copy of the original. The aim is a clear, accurate photograph, not a product that looks different from what arrives.

Some Amazon MAIN images require a pure white background, RGB 255, 255, 255. Requirements and exceptions vary by category and marketplace, so verify the current rules in official Seller Central help and your category style guide before uploading.

Set up near a window

Choose a bright window with indirect daylight. Direct sun can make hard shadows and bright patches that are difficult to edit. Turn off nearby lamps if their color makes one side yellow and the other blue. A cloudy day or a sheer curtain can soften the light; do not place hot lamps against fabric or paper.

Use a large sheet of matte white paper or a clean white sweep curved from the tabletop up behind the product. The curve removes a distracting horizon line. Move the product a little forward from the backdrop so it does not cast a strong shadow on the upright section. A second piece of white card opposite the window can bounce light into the darker side.

  • Wipe the item, backdrop and phone lens. Remove dust that is not part of the product.
  • Keep the setup stable with a small tripod or a stack of books, and use a timer to reduce movement.
  • For reflective items, look for reflections of the phone, window and room. Move the setup or use larger white cards to control them.

Photograph with your phone

Use the rear camera at its normal lens setting where possible. Digital zoom can discard detail, so move the camera instead. Tap the product to focus and adjust exposure until the item retains its highlights and texture. A background that looks slightly gray is easier to correct than a product whose pale details have been blown out.

Shoot the full item with space around all sides, then take alternative angles and close-ups. Keep the camera roughly level with the product for a straightforward view; changing the height can distort proportions. Check the screen at full size for blur, clipped edges and readable labels. Photograph each sold variant separately when color or shape changes.

Edit the background carefully

Save an untouched original and work on a copy. Correct exposure and white balance gently while comparing the product with the real item in neutral light. If the destination calls for pure white, select or mask the background and set it to RGB 255, 255, 255. Use an editor’s color sampler to check several areas, including corners and near the product; a white-looking screen alone is not a reliable measurement.

Zoom in around handles, fine edges, transparent materials and pale surfaces. Refine the mask so the background does not leave a gray halo or cut into the item. Compare the silhouette with the original, especially around small openings. A subtle, natural shadow can ground an item where the destination permits it; do not paint in a shadow that changes the apparent shape or hides a required white area. Check the relevant rules first.

Review before export

  • View against white and a temporary contrasting color to find stray pixels, jagged edges and missing details. Restore white before exporting.
  • Check the actual product color, markings and texture against the original. Avoid smoothing scratches that represent its real condition.
  • Inspect the background with the sampler after resizing and export; compression can change near-white pixels.
  • Preview the complete image on a phone and at full size. Confirm the crop leaves the entire item visible where required.

Common mistakes include overexposing a white product, using a wrinkled backdrop, mixing light colors and relying on an automatic cutout without reviewing it. If a glossy or transparent object is difficult to isolate, return to the lighting setup and make another exposure before attempting more retouching.
